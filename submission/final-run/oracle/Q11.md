# Q11 - Hotter wells (oracle / ground truth)

Asker: Olivia Grant. Question: "Which region of the field consistently runs hotter?" Today = 2027-01-15.
Computed over all 125,198 inspections (Oct 2022 - Jan 2027, 600 wells), `data_correction` applied (WELL-0201, WELL-0213), `type_history` used for the type on the inspection date (only WELL-0010 has one: T200 until 2026-11-19, T350 from 2026-11-20).

## Expected answer

**No region genuinely runs hotter.** The raw numbers point at **Southeast (Block 31, then Block 32)**, but that is a confound, not a real effect:
1. **Ambient is the same everywhere.** Mean ambient air temperature per area is 21.47 - 21.55 C (spread 0.08 C); month by month the areas are within a few tenths of a degree. There is no geographic temperature gradient (correlation of well mean ambient with latitude 0.05, with longitude -0.01). The area centres are only about 0.5 deg of latitude and 0.45 deg of longitude apart. So "region" does not change the weather in this dataset; the large seasonal swing (8 C in January to 36 C in July) hits every region identically.
2. **Wellhead temperature is dominated by well type**, which differs between regions: producing readings average wellhead - ambient of **T200 25.9 C, T350 31.0 C, T500 36.2 C** (raw wellhead 47.5 / 52.5 / 57.7 C). Southeast Block 31 is 58 % T500 wells (29 of 50; 55 % of readings), Southeast Block 32 25 %, Northwest Block 12 23 % (34 of 150 wells), the NE and SW blocks about 0-6 %. The ranking by raw wellhead temperature, SE-31 52.9 > SE-32 50.8 > NW-12 50.1 > NE-07 48.8 > SW-23 48.6 > NE-08 48.3 > SW-24 47.7 C, is exactly the ranking of T500 share.
3. **Compare like with like and the difference vanishes.** Producing wells, wellhead minus ambient, by area and type:

| Area | T200 | T350 | T500 |
|---|---|---|---|
| NE-07 | 26.04 | 30.95 | 36.47 (1 well) |
| NE-08 | 25.89 | 31.07 | 36.59 (1 well) |
| NW-12 | 25.91 | 30.95 | 36.22 |
| SE-31 | 25.84 | 30.90 | 36.25 |
| SE-32 | 25.95 | 30.94 | 36.25 |
| SW-23 | 25.96 | 30.96 | 36.18 (4 wells) |
| SW-24 | 26.01 | 30.98 | no T500 wells |

Every cell in a column is within about 0.3 C (T500 NE cells are single wells). Type-adjusted delta (reading minus the field mean for its type), producing readings, by area: NE-07 +0.05, NE-08 +0.01, NW-12 -0.03, SE-31 -0.03, SE-32 -0.01, SW-23 0.00, SW-24 +0.04 C. By compass region: NE +0.03, NW -0.03, SE -0.01, SW +0.02 C. Well-level residual spread (std) is only 0.29 C.
4. **Consistency over time:** SE-31 has the highest raw wellhead temperature in **52 of 52 months** (and every year 2022-2026 the raw top three is SE-31, SE-32, NW-12) - the "consistently hotter" the question is baited with - but after the type adjustment the month-by-month leader is random (SE-31 6 months, NE-07 12, SW-24 12, SW-23 10, NE-08 6, SE-32 4, NW-12 2) and monthly differences are +-0.5 C noise. Annual spread of adjusted means across areas: 0.42 (2022), 0.25, 0.19, 0.23, 0.15 (2026) C.

**Recommended defensible answer to give Olivia:** "Taken literally (raw wellhead temperature) Southeast Block 31 is hotter every single month, followed by Southeast Block 32 and Northwest Block 12 - but that is because those blocks hold most of the T500 ESP wells, which run about 10 C above the T200 rod-lift wells whatever the location. Ambient air is the same across the field (21.5 C average, <0.1 C difference) and, within a well type, wellhead-minus-ambient is the same in every block (within about 0.3 C). So no region consistently runs hotter in any meaningful way; compare wellhead minus ambient within type (and against the type's producing threshold), not raw temperature." It is acceptable to say Southeast looks hotter on raw numbers, if it explains the type-mix cause. Asking a clarifying question ("hotter meaning wellhead, wellhead above ambient, or against its threshold?") is also acceptable provided the confound is still named.

## What "hotter" should mean (the judgement)

Use **wellhead minus ambient at the time of the reading** (the quantity the manuals use to judge production: T200.md 9.3, T350.md section 3, T500.md section 3 and "Never judge production from absolute wellhead temperature") and compare **within well type** (or standardise to a common type mix). Also useful: share of readings at or above the type's producing threshold (about 94 - 96 % in every area: NE-07 94.9, NE-08 95.3, NW-12 95.2, SE-31 96.1, SE-32 95.9, SW-23 94.8, SW-24 93.6 %; with the small SW-24 deficit being operational downtime, not heat) and alarm limits (T200 85 C, T350/T500 95 C). Absolute wellhead temperature is confounded by (a) ambient air, which swings seasonally by ~28 C, and (b) well type.

## Traps

1. **Simpson's paradox / type mix:** raw wellhead temperature ranks regions by T500 share; the answer "Southeast Block 31" (or "Southeast") without the caveat is the trap.
2. **Ambient confound:** wellhead temperature moves with season (monthly mean wellhead 33 - 68 C); a comparison needs ambient subtracted. Here ambient does not differ by region, so subtracting it does not change the ranking; the type mix does.
3. **"Consistently"**: requires looking over time (month/year), not a single snapshot. Raw: SE-31 first every month (52/52). Adjusted: nobody.
4. Using the T200 15 C threshold for everything, or ignoring `type_history` (WELL-0010 converted to T350 on 2026-11-20) distorts per-type comparisons marginally.
5. Do not pick on the single-well T500 cells in the NE blocks.
6. `data_correction` readings (WELL-0201, WELL-0213) are tiny in a 125k-reading dataset; they do not change the answer but must not be silently ignored if wells are listed.
7. Do not interpret the SW-24 lower share of producing readings (93.6 %) as "cooler running" - it reflects idle/shut-in readings.

## Confidence

High (90%) that the data contains no genuine regional heat effect and that the raw Southeast result is a well-type artefact (all comparisons are direct aggregations; the effect sizes are two orders of magnitude apart: ~4 C raw gap vs ~0.05 C adjusted gap). Medium on how the grader frames the "right" final sentence (it may accept "Southeast looks hotter on raw numbers, but it is the T500 mix; no region is really hotter" or a clarifying question that names the confound).
