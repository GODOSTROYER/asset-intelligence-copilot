# Q1 oracle - "Map the reservoir pressure across the field" (Michael Turner)

## Expected answer
**Cannot be done: the dataset contains no reservoir pressure data (and no wellhead/tubing/casing/bottom-hole pressure readings) for any well.** A correct answer must:
1. Say plainly that there is no reservoir pressure (or any live/periodic pressure measurement) in the data, so a pressure map cannot be drawn, and NOT fabricate, interpolate or infer pressure (e.g. from wellhead temperature, production state or well type).
2. Not draw a heat map / contour of invented values. (A map of anything else must be clearly labelled as something else.)
3. State what pressure-related facts DO exist, and that they are not reservoir pressure:
   - Flowline pressure-test figures inside repair reports (WS-05 section "Testing and return to service"): "Pressure-tested the repaired section at N psi (1.25 x the flowline's maximum operating pressure of M psi) and held it for 4 hours". This is a one-off test of a repaired flowline section, not a reservoir or wellhead reading. Found in 2,432 reports on 561 wells; MOP values 150 to 320 psi, test pressures 188 to 400 psi.
   - Design limits in the well-type manuals: T200 "Maximum allowable wellhead working pressure: 3,000 psi" (T200.md 9.1); T350 wellhead rating 3,000 psi (T200) vs 5,000 psi (T350) (T350.md section 2 table). These are equipment ratings, not measurements.
   - Qualitative mentions: two operator notes "high pressure alarm on the separator" (WELL-0422 CTX-0422-003 and WELL-0481 CTX-0481-006, both 2027-01-14): an alarm event, no value; repair-report lines about bleeding off pressure / "pressure tested" (no values), 74 ESP wellhead pressure tests with no number; Field Work SOP WS-02/WS-05 and pressure-testing rules text.
4. Optionally offer genuinely available alternatives (clearly labelled): wellhead temperature and ambient temperature per inspection (producing/not-producing status by the manual rule), spill volume, downtime, etc.; and say what would be needed (a pressure/SCADA/well-test feed or static pressure surveys).
5. Also challenge the premise lightly: "all wells feed one reservoir" is not stated anywhere in the data; there is no reservoir/pool/formation field in metadata or manuals (only a generic "Lifts fluid from the reservoir" phrase in T200.md equipment table).

A regex over all keys for press|psi|bhp|whp|thp|chp returned nothing. 125,198 inspections, 17,415 maintenance jobs, 1,887 context notes, 328 regulatory events, 600 metadata files.

## Rules applied
- No SOP defines reservoir pressure; the correct behaviour is the benchmark's "some cannot be fully answered from the data" rule (final-questions.md preamble): check reasoning and evidence, do not invent.

## Traps and edge cases
- Trap 1: presenting flowline MOP / test pressures (150-320 psi, 188-400 psi) as "reservoir pressure". They are flowline integrity-test numbers on repaired sections, only on wells with a repair report, and are per job, not a field state.
- Trap 2: mapping wellhead temperature as a "pressure proxy" or mapping the 3,000/5,000 psi ratings as measurements.
- Trap 3: a "map" visual is requested in spirit. A correct answer may show a map only of what exists (well locations), with no pressure coloring, or no map at all. A pressure-coloured map would be a failure.
- Trap 4: the two "high pressure alarm on the separator" notes tempt a "high pressure at WELL-0422 / WELL-0481" claim; they carry no number and concern separator, not reservoir.
- The sole pressure unit used is psi.
