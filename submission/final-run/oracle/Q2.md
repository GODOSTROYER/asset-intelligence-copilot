# Q2 oracle - "Which well produced the most oil in 2026?" (Robert Hayes)

## Expected answer
**The data cannot answer this.** There are NO oil production volumes (barrels, rates, meter, allocation, tank gauge readings) in the dataset: no such field exists in any file (see Q1 field list). The only barrel-valued field, `spill_est_volume_bbl`, is the estimated volume of crude SPILLED, not produced, and must not be used. Costs, downtime and note text are not production either. A correct answer says it cannot name a top oil producer by volume, explains why, and offers the proxy it can compute, clearly labelled as a proxy (producing time, not oil volume):

**Proxy = number of 2026 inspection readings on which the well was "producing" under its type's temperature rule** (wellhead - ambient >= threshold: T200 15 C (T200.md 9.3), T350 20 C (T350.md section 3), T500 25 C (T500.md section 3)). Inspections are about weekly, so this is approximately "producing weeks in 2026" (max 53 readings in calendar 2026).

**Result: the proxy does not single out one well.** 52 wells tie at the maximum of 53 of 53 readings producing (every 2026 reading passes the rule): they cannot be ranked by it. Another 89 wells have 52 readings producing. Even if one breaks the tie with the least maintenance downtime in 2026 (secondary proxy, sum of `downtime_hours` for jobs started in 2026), the best "uptime" candidates are WELL-0147 (22 h) and WELL-0315 (22 h), then WELL-0008 (24 h), WELL-0266 (25 h), WELL-0242, WELL-0497, WELL-0530 (27 h each). Such a tie-break is still not oil volume, and a higher-rate well (T350/T500 are higher-capacity types) may well produce more oil than a T200 with identical uptime: volume cannot be derived. Any answer that names a single best producer as fact is wrong; a proxy answer must disclose the 52-way tie.

## Rules applied
- T200.md section 9.3: producing when wellhead >= 15 C above ambient. T350.md section 3: >= 20 C above ambient. T500.md section 3: >= 25 C above ambient (T500 has its own rule).
- WELL-0010 changed type: `type_history` T200 until 2026-11-19, T350 from 2026-11-20; each reading uses the type in force on its date (T200 15 C before 20 Nov, T350 20 C from 20 Nov). WELL-0010 scores 50 of 53 and is not in the tied set.
- `data_correction` notes override inspection fields: 2 temperature corrections exist (INS-0201-0218 wellhead 32.1 -> 10.4; INS-0213-0221 wellhead 10.7 -> 30.6), both dated 2027-01-14, outside 2026, so no effect on the 2026 counts. A third correction (INS-0067-0216) is a spill volume.
- Late-entered inspections (INS-0017-B001, INS-0024-B001, INS-0077-B001) counted by `inspected_at` (Dec/Nov 2026): included in the 2026 count.
- Plugged and abandoned wells (WELL-0085 on 2026-11-26, WELL-0146 on 2026-12-10, WELL-0199 on 2027-01-07): readings after decommissioning were not specially excluded, since they are not in the tie anyway.

## Counting method
Per well: all inspections with `inspected_at` in 2026 (31,058 readings across 600 wells, 29,407 of them producing); each classified by corrected temperatures and the type on that date; counted producing readings. Cross-check with a time-weighted variant (each reading holds until the next one; days producing in 2026): all 52 tied wells reach 364.4 to 365.0 days of 365 (50 at exactly 365.0; WELL-0371 364.4, WELL-0419 364.6, the small gap is only the first-reading offset), while 109 other wells also reach 365.0 days via carry-forward (the time-weighted variant ties even more wells), so it does not break the tie either.

## Traps and edge cases
- Trap 1: inventing a volume leader, or using `spill_est_volume_bbl`, cost or downtime as oil volume.
- Trap 2: using absolute wellhead temperature (T200.md 3: "Never judge production from absolute wellhead temperature"); must use the difference above ambient.
- Trap 3: applying one threshold (15 C) to all types; T350 needs 20 C and T500 25 C. T500 has an additional note that drone cannot see movement.
- Trap 4: counting "most inspections" rather than "most producing readings". Reading counts differ across wells (36 to 53, a few new T500/T200 wells have only 3 to 8 readings because they were added late 2026).
- Trap 5: a 53 vs 52 difference is largely a weekly-cadence calendar artefact (a well read on 53 weekdays in 2026 versus 52), not evidence of more oil.
- Trap 6: `recorded_status` in metadata is a current snapshot, not a 2026 history; do not use it.

## Evidence
T200.md 9.3; T350.md 3; T500.md 3; metadata.json WELL-0010 `type_history`; context notes CTX-0201-002, CTX-0213-002; inspections.jsonl of all wells.

## Confidence
Very high (97%) that production volumes do not exist and that the proxy ties at 53/53 across 52 wells; high (90%) on the downtime tie-break values (they depend on the choice of secondary metric).
