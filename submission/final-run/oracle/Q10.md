# Q10 oracle - What are we waiting on? (Michael Turner)

Question: *"When a well stays shut in longer than the repair itself, what are we usually waiting on: parts, crews, contractors or weather? Break it down by area for jobs started in 2026, with the typical number of extra days, and tell me if any area stands out."*

Computed from the raw test dataset (600 wells, today = 2027-01-15) by script over every `maintenance-reports/*.md`.

## 1. Where the waiting reason is recorded (the key finding)

- The waiting reason AND the number of extra days are written in the maintenance report, section `## What happened`, last sentence. They are NOT in `maintenance.jsonl` (no reason field) and NOT reliably in `context.jsonl` operator notes.
- Six sentence templates exist (2,325 reports across all years; 634 for jobs started in 2026):
  1. `The well stayed shut in for about N days longer than the repair itself because heavy rain made the lease road impassable for the service truck.` -> **Weather**
  2. `... because the replacement part was back-ordered from the supplier.` -> **Parts**
  3. `... because the job had to wait for a weekday contractor slot.` -> **Contractors**
  4. `... because the crew was committed to a higher-priority spill response elsewhere in the field.` -> **Crews**
  5. `Sandstone Rod & Pump's workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about N days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.` (also Coyote Ridge Well Services) -> **Contractors** (contractor's rig)
  6. `The company workover rig was busy on other wells, so the well waited about N days for it; the work itself took about 2 days.` -> **Crews** (own rig/crew)
- Interim reports (jobs still in progress on 15 Jan 2027) use future wording `is expected to stay shut in for about N days longer ...` (and, for rig waits, an unattributed `the workover rig is busy on other wells`; classify by the job's `contractor` field). Two such interim jobs started in 2026 (MNT-0404-034, MNT-0496-033); they are counted, and excluding them changes nothing material.
- How extra days are measured: the number N in the sentence (for rig-busy sentences N = days waited for the rig; the work itself is ~2 days). Cross-check in structured data: `downtime_hours` equals `completed_at - started_at` for these jobs, i.e. the job span INCLUDES the wait, so repair-only time = downtime_hours/24 - N. The extra days cannot be derived from timestamps alone; they must be read from the report text. Non-producing inspections or notes are not needed (and are not exact enough).
- Population: every job with `started_at` in 2026 whose report has one of these sentences = **634 jobs** (of 4,360 jobs started in 2026 across all wells; the rest had no recorded wait). Area = the well's `area_id`. Waiting jobs that started in 2027 (42) are excluded.

### Trap: decoy operator notes
`context.jsonl` has generic notes `Waiting on replacement parts from the vendor; well stays shut in until they arrive.` (70) and `Workover rig delayed by weather; restart pushed back about a week.` (93). They carry no day counts and their reason does NOT match the report of the nearest job (only about one in five agree, i.e. essentially random). Do not use them as the source; the maintenance report is authoritative. A copilot that counts these notes gets wrong shares.

### Sensitivity
- Excluding the 2 interim (future-tense) jobs: 632 jobs; shares move by under 1 point (Contractors 32.6%, Crews 26.9%, Weather 22.0%, Parts 18.5%).
- Crews vs Contractors is a classification choice. The oracle follows the report wording: contractor named / contractor slot -> Contractors; company rig / own crew on spill response -> Crews. If instead every 'workover rig busy' sentence were called Crews (134 jobs: company 58, contractor 76), Crews would be 244 (38%) and Contractors 131 (21%). A good answer states its classification.
- Overall median extra days is about 9 for every reason: waits are widely spread (1 to ~39 days, quartiles roughly 3-7 and 16-22), so 'typical' does not separate the reasons.

## 3. Does any area stand out?

**Expected judgement: no area clearly stands out.** The honest answer is that the mix is broadly the same everywhere; the copilot should not invent a hotspot.

- Chi-square test of area x reason: chi2 = 10.3 on 18 degrees of freedom (p about 0.92). The differences are within chance.
- Median extra days per area are 8 to 10 days everywhere (NE-08 lowest at 8, SE-31 highest at 10).
- Largest apparent deviations (small-sample noise; mention as 'worth watching' at most): Southeast Block 31 has the highest Contractors share (42% of 40 jobs, median 14 d) and Southwest Block 24 also 41% of 66; Northeast Block 7 parts waits have the longest median (15 d, only 15 jobs); Southwest Block 23 parts median 12.5 d (16 jobs); Southeast Block 32 has the heaviest tail (mean 13.2 d vs median 9).
- Volume differs mainly with well count. Waiting jobs per well (2026): SW-23 72/63 = 1.14, SW-24 66/63 = 1.05, NE-07 64/62 = 1.03, NE-08 80/62 = 1.29, SE-31 40/50 = 0.80, NW-12 172/150 = 1.15, SE-32 140/150 = 0.93. Northwest Block 12 (172) and Southeast Block 32 (140) have the most waiting jobs because they have 150 wells each; the per-well rate is highest in NE-08 (1.29) and lowest in SE-31 (0.80), nothing dramatic.
- Fleet-wide answer: **Contractors** is the most common reason (33%: weekday contractor slot 131 + contractor rig busy 76), then **Crews** (27%), **Weather** (22%), **Parts** (18%, the smallest share). Typical (median) extra wait about 9 days whatever the reason.

## 4. What the visual should be

A table (section 2) plus a stacked bar chart: x = 7 areas, stack = Parts / Crews / Contractors / Weather, y = share of the area's waiting jobs (or counts). Contractors is the largest segment in 5 of 7 areas (tallest in SE-31 and SW-24); Crews is marginally ahead in NE-08 (31% vs 30%) and NW-12 (31% vs 30%).

## 5. Scoring notes / confidence

- Must-haves: (a) reads the reason from the maintenance reports, not the decoy operator notes; (b) restricts to jobs started in 2026 (`started_at`, not completion; some 2026 jobs finish in 2027); (c) per-area table with share and median extra days; (d) states that no area stands out (or at most flags SE-31/SW-24 contractors and NE-07 parts as small-sample, low confidence); (e) says reasons are classified from free text and states the Crews/Contractors choice.
- Evidence IDs (2026 examples): MNT-0045-027 (WELL-0045, SW-23, Crews), MNT-0072-029 (Weather), MNT-0461-022 (Parts), MNT-0199-025 (Contractors); interim: MNT-0404-034, MNT-0496-033. Template exemplars from other years: MNT-0599-021 (weather, 2 d), MNT-0600-028 (parts, 9 d), MNT-0599-030 (contractor slot, 8 d), MNT-0599-015 (spill-response crew, 8 d), MNT-0600-016 (Sandstone rig, 21 d), MNT-0597-016 (company rig, 9 d).
- Traps: decoy notes; started_at year vs completion year; interim 'expected' wording; the 42 waiting jobs started in 2027 must be excluded; for rig-busy sentences 'extra' is the wait (work itself ~2 days); the wait is already inside `downtime_hours`, do not add it again.
- Confidence: high on counts (deterministic matching of fixed templates; every 2026 waiting job classified); medium on the Crews/Contractors boundary (judgement); high on 'no area stands out'.
